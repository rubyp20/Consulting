## Module 15 — Quiz answers

1. **Labour arbitrage** earns margin by delivering the same work from a lower-cost location with process standardisation; the unit of sale is labour and scale economics are weak. **Automation arbitrage** earns margin from the gap between a price still anchored to labour units and a cost base that has partly become software with near-zero marginal cost. Under an unchanged FTE-based contract the provider captures all of it, and has a disincentive to automate because automation reduces billable FTE. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer says the pricing model, not goodwill, decides who captures the gap.

2. **New price EUR 18.0m (24 x 0.75); new margin EUR 5.0m (18.0 - 13.0), or 27.8% of price and 38.5% on cost.** Current margin is EUR 4.0m (24 - 20), 16.7% of price and 20% on cost. The provider's absolute and percentage margin both rise even after a 25% price cut, which shows that a 25% cut leaves a large share of the automation gain with the provider. Manager-level benchmark: the strong answer proposes a step-down schedule so later gains flow to the client, and asks whether the EUR 13m includes amortised transition cost.

Any four of: **decision-level accuracy and quality SLAs with measurement method and sample size; model and prompt change notification with re-validation evidence and client veto for Tier 1; audit and validation access for the client's second line and regulators; data-use restrictions (no training on client data without consent, residency, retention); liability and indemnity for decision errors reflecting regulatory exposure; exit and portability covering golden datasets, prompts, exception taxonomies and transition assistance; benefit-sharing and step-down mechanics with a frozen baseline; minimum human-oversight commitments by risk tier.** Manager-level benchmark: explains why each matters in model-risk or outsourcing-rule terms, not just as a list.

4. **Because (a) retained roles are more senior and more expensive per head** (exception adjudicators, QA auditors, agent supervisors), **and (b) new cost lines appear** (AI run cost, agent engineering and evaluation, amortised transition cost, higher risk cost). A third reason: fixed costs such as licences and management do not scale down proportionally. In the worked example, 57.5% headcount reduction gave a 35% cost reduction. Manager-level benchmark: quantifies the gap and uses it to set client expectations before the provider does.

5. **No.** The bank remains accountable for the outcomes of models used in its business. The relevant UK references are PRA SS1/23 (model risk management principles, effective May 2024) for the model itself and PRA SS2/21 (outsourcing and third-party risk management) for the arrangement, with the EBA outsourcing guidelines (2019) and DORA (from January 2025) relevant for EU-regulated entities, all `[VERIFY]`. In practice the provider's agent must be in the bank's model inventory with validation evidence, monitoring and audit access. Manager-level benchmark: distinguishes outsourcing the operation of a model from outsourcing accountability, and names the SMF accountability under SS1/23.

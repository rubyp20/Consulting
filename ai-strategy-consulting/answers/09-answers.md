## Module 9 — Quiz answers

**1. Vendor case-study haircut.** Apply roughly 50–70%, treating it as vendor claim or benchmark-from-another-bank evidence. The other bank's baseline, volumes, process design and data quality differ from the client's, and vendors publish their best outcomes. Manager-level benchmark: names the evidence-haircut ladder, states that the haircut falls as the client runs its own PoC and pilot, and proposes the pilot measurement that would justify lifting it.

**2. Capacity release vs cash-releasing.** Cash-releasing benefit reduces an actual cost line (payroll, contractor, vendor) that an owner commits to cut. Capacity release frees hours while headcount stays flat, so it only becomes bookable if (a) roles are removed through attrition or contractor release, (b) staff are redeployed into measurable revenue or risk work, or (c) the freed capacity absorbs planned volume growth that would otherwise have required funded hires. Manager-level benchmark: insists on a signed headcount bridge by quarter and reports unbooked capacity separately.

**3. NPV and payback.** Discount factors at 10%: 1.000, 0.909, 0.826, 0.751, 0.683. PVs: −3.00, −0.45, 1.49, 1.50, 1.37. NPV ≈ GBP 0.90m. Cumulative undiscounted: −3.0, −3.5, −1.7, +0.3, so payback falls in Year 3; interpolating 1.7/2.0 = 0.85 gives about 2.85 years. Manager-level benchmark: shows the factors, rounds sensibly, and comments that payback near three years is at the edge of a typical bank CFO's comfort range.

**4. Combined ramp.** 0.50 × 0.80 × 0.75 = 0.30, so the Year 1 ramp is 30%, not 50%. A case assuming 50% in Year 1 overstates Year 1 benefit by two-thirds and will miss its first-year target, damaging credibility for later funding. Manager-level benchmark: explains that each component is individually plausible but multiplicative, and ties coverage to the IT roadmap and adoption to the change plan.

**5. CFO challenges and pre-emptions.** Any four of: double counting (reconcile across programme benefit registers and show net-of-overlap); baseline inflation (trailing twelve months, fully loaded cost with HR sign-off); ramp optimism (decompose into coverage, adoption, effectiveness tied to roadmap); run-cost omission (full TCO including inference, human review, model risk, refresh); attribution (control group or phased rollout agreed before go-live); capacity release (signed headcount bridge); terminal value (no benefit growth beyond volume, include refresh cost); downside (probability-weighted NPV and gated funding). Manager-level benchmark: gives the pre-emption as a specific artefact or method, not a general reassurance.

## Module 13 — Quiz answers

1. **Talent, money and decision rights.** A CoE holds all three centrally; hub-and-spoke splits them (hub: platform money, standards, scarce talent; spokes: use-case money, business decision rights, embedded delivery); federated gives nearly all three to business units and keeps policy centrally. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer also names what stays central even in a federated model (model inventory, tiering, validation standards).

2. **Four validators in total.** 14 systems at one validator per five systems is 2.8, rounded up to 3 for full validation, plus 1 for the remaining 26 lighter-touch systems, giving 4. The binding assumption is the effort norm per Tier 1 or Tier 2 validation (initial validation plus annual re-validation plus event-driven re-validation on model or prompt changes); if agentic systems change frequently, the ratio tightens and the number rises. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer rounds up explicitly, states the assumption, and notes that validation is often the critical path on the roadmap rather than engineering.

3. **Business-funded.** Each unit funds its own use cases and no one funds cross-cutting platform and foundations. The fix is a hybrid: central funding for platform and risk functions, business funding for use cases, and a stage-gated central pool for new domains, with a benefits register so the centrally funded hub is credited for benefits landing in business P&Ls. Manager-level benchmark: mentions chargeback as a later-stage discipline once platform services are mature.

4. **Roughly 10% algorithms and models, 20% technology and data, 70% people, process and business change (BCG, 2023–24).** It implies that change, adoption, process redesign and domain SME capacity should dominate the resourcing plan; an operating model whose headcount is mostly data scientists and engineers is inverted relative to where value is created and lost. Manager-level benchmark: attributes it to BCG, treats it as a heuristic not a law, and converts it into a staffing check.

5. **Because otherwise each spoke rebuilds the same components (gateway, retrieval, evaluation, guardrails), producing duplicated spend, inconsistent controls and no central model inventory.** The hub should ship the minimum platform (gateway, retrieval service, eval harness) so spokes build on it from day one; the sequencing is what makes the target state credible. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer ties this to regulatory consequences (fragmented standards visible to the PRA) as well as cost.

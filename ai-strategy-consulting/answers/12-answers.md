## Module 12 — Quiz answers

**1. Annex III points.** Point 5(b): creditworthiness assessment or credit scoring of natural persons, excluding fraud detection. Point 5(c): risk assessment and pricing for life and health insurance for natural persons. Not high-risk under Annex III: AML transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, dispute resolution, corporate lending to legal entities, internal knowledge assistants (though chatbots carry Article 50 disclosure duties). Manager-level benchmark: adds that employment-related AI (point 4) catches HR analytics and that all dates are subject to the November 2025 omnibus proposal [VERIFY].

**2. SS1/23 principles and definition.** Model identification and model risk classification; governance; model development, implementation and use; independent model validation; model risk mitigants. The model definition includes qualitative or expert-judgement inputs and qualitative outputs, which brings LLM-based systems inside model risk management, so the debate shifts from "is it a model" to "which tier and how do we validate the system in use". Manager-level benchmark: names the SMF accountability and the self-assessment expectation, and notes the initial scope of firms with internal model permissions [VERIFY].

**3. Disparate impact.** Ratio = 47% / 62% = 0.758. Below 0.80, so it fails the four-fifths screen and warrants investigation; failing the screen is evidence of adverse impact, not proof of unlawful discrimination, and the bank must examine whether legitimate, non-proxy factors explain the gap. Manager-level benchmark: proposes a second metric such as equalised odds and notes the impossibility results when base rates differ.

**4. LLM rationale.** A generated rationale is post-hoc text produced by the same model, not a faithful trace of the factors that determined the output; research (Turpin et al., 2023) shows stated reasoning can diverge from the actual drivers. The architectural answer is to keep the decision in an explainable component (scorecard, constrained model, rules) that the agent calls as a tool, and to require the LLM's text to cite verifiable evidence for every fact rather than to justify the decision. Manager-level benchmark: distinguishes citation grounding (checkable) from rationale generation (useful for review, not an explanation).

**5. NIST functions.** Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. GenAI Profile categories include confabulation, information integrity, information security, intellectual property, harmful bias and homogenisation, data privacy, and value chain and component integration. Manager-level benchmark: explains that Govern is cross-cutting and uses the RMF as a crosswalk to ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act rather than as a separate programme.

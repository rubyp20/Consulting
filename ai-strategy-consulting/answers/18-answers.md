## Module 18 — Quiz answers

1. **Revised NPV is about EUR 25m.** Year 3 net falls from 21 to 3 (42 − 18 = 24 benefit, minus 12 investment and 9 run); year 4 net falls from 34 to 28 (42 − 4 − 10). NPV = −15/1.10 − 5/1.21 + 3/1.331 + 28/1.4641 + 34/1.6105 = −13.6 − 4.1 + 2.3 + 19.1 + 21.1 = EUR 24.8m. Allowing for rounding in the module's figures, an answer between EUR 24m and 26m is acceptable; the point is that a one-year wave 2 slip removes roughly 40% of NPV. Manager-level benchmark: rebuilds the table rather than guessing, states the sensitivity as a share of base NPV, and names the mitigation (start KYC data remediation in month 1).

2. **D4, error tolerance and reversibility, decided it.** A missed beneficial owner is a regulatory failure that is hard to detect after approval and expensive to remediate, so despite a composite score on the automation boundary the task was placed in assist with mandatory human confirmation for layered structures. The most relevant SS1/23 principle is Principle 5, model risk mitigants, which expects compensating controls where model limitations are known; Principle 1 (classification) is also acceptable if argued through materiality. Manager-level benchmark: says explicitly that the score informs and the risk dimension decides, and connects the design choice to a named regulatory principle.

3. **Capacity conversion at 60% cuts NPV from USD 20.2m to 7.1m; a straight-through rate of 35% cuts it to 13.1m.** The conversion driver is larger because it scales the entire USD 12m effort benefit, which is 77% of total benefit, whereas the straight-through rate moves only part of that benefit and leaves the loss and complaint benefits intact. The message to the CFO is that the business case is a workforce case first and a technology case second. Manager-level benchmark: quotes both NPV figures, explains the mechanism rather than only the ranking, and draws the implication for where management attention goes.

4. **Per dispute: mid-tier 42,000 × 0.80/1m + 4,200 × 4/1m = USD 0.051; frontier 18,000 × 3/1m + 1,800 × 15/1m = USD 0.081; total USD 0.13, plus 30% for retries and evaluation = USD 0.17; 600,000 disputes = USD 0.10m a year against USD 4.4m run cost.** The implication is that model choice should be presented to the client on accuracy, controllability, data residency and vendor stability, not on price per token, and that integration and people dominate TCO. Manager-level benchmark: does the arithmetic from memory of the shape (tokens × price, tiered routing, overhead), labels it an order-of-magnitude estimate, and makes the presentational point unprompted.

5. **The low-value write-off pool is a policy decision to credit disputes under a threshold rather than investigate them, so better handling does not change it; counting it would be claiming a benefit the platform cannot cause.** Excluding it signals to a CFO that the team understands causal attribution and will not inflate the case, which buys credibility for the benefits that are claimed.

# Module 0 — Diagnostic model answers

Score 0, 1 or 2 per question against these. The "benchmark" line describes what a manager-level AI consultant's answer contains.

**Q1. Tool choice.** A rules engine for the eligibility decision itself: the logic is codified (scheme reason codes, time limits, amount thresholds), the inputs are structured, and the regulator needs reproducibility and auditability, which deterministic logic gives and an LLM does not. LLMs add value upstream and downstream: extracting facts from unstructured cardholder correspondence and merchant evidence, classifying the dispute reason, drafting the representment or customer letter, and summarising the case for the agent.
Benchmark: names the rules engine first, explains why (determinism, audit), then positions the LLM on the unstructured edges, and mentions that the combination is the typical production pattern.

**Q2. Context window.** The context window is the maximum number of tokens the model can attend to in one call (prompt plus generated output). Cost scales roughly linearly with input tokens and latency rises with length; attention cost grows faster than linearly in naive implementations. Stuffing the whole file degrades retrieval quality ("lost in the middle" effects), increases hallucination risk through irrelevant context, and makes every call expensive regardless of how much of the file is relevant. Retrieval (RAG) selects the relevant passages instead.
Benchmark: gives the definition, the cost and latency consequence, the quality consequence, and names RAG as the alternative design.

**Q3. Supervisor vs orchestrator-worker.** Supervisor (hierarchical): a supervising agent routes the task to specialised sub-agents, evaluates their outputs and decides next steps; sub-agents may themselves have sub-agents. Orchestrator-worker: a central orchestrator decomposes a task into sub-tasks, dispatches them to identical or interchangeable workers (often in parallel) and merges results. Use supervisor for KYB where sub-tasks differ in nature (ownership resolution, adverse media, document verification) and need judgement about what to do next. Use orchestrator-worker for parallel bulk work such as screening 2,000 names against lists or summarising 50 documents the same way.
Benchmark: distinguishes heterogeneity-plus-judgement from homogeneity-plus-parallelism, and gives a BFSI example of each.

**Q4. Evaluation claims.** Ask: accurate against what reference (who labelled the golden set, how large, how representative of our alert mix)? Accurate on what unit (per narrative, per fact, per field)? Measured when and on which model version, and what is the false-positive versus false-negative split? Insist on faithfulness/groundedness (every claim traceable to source evidence) and a hallucination rate or unsupported-claim rate, plus precision and recall on the key extracted fields rather than a single accuracy figure.
Benchmark: challenges the denominator and the labelling, asks for a confusion matrix style view, and names faithfulness explicitly.

**Q5. Attribution.** 10-20-70 is BCG: roughly 10% of the effort and value is in algorithms, 20% in technology and data, 70% in people, process and business change. "Rewired" (2023) is by McKinsey authors Lamarre, Smaje and Zemmel; the six capabilities are business-led digital roadmap, talent, operating model, technology, data, and adoption and scaling.
Benchmark: both attributions correct, six capabilities listed, and a one-line point of view on what each framework is useful for.

**Q6. High value, low feasibility quadrant.** Do not discard them and do not force them into wave one. Decompose each into the specific feasibility blockers (data access, integration, regulatory sign-off, model capability), cost the removal of each blocker, and treat the blockers as enablers in the roadmap. Some of these use cases become wave two or three once the enabler lands; others reveal that the feasibility score was really a data problem shared across several use cases, which strengthens the case for a data investment. Junior mistake: labelling them "strategic bets" and parking them with no owner, or sliding them into wave one because a sponsor likes them.
Benchmark: explicit decomposition into blockers, blockers become roadmap enablers, and a sequencing logic.

**Q7. Cost arithmetic.** Input: 60,000 x 40,000 = 2.4 billion tokens x USD 3 per million = USD 7,200. Output: 60,000 x 4,000 = 240 million tokens x USD 15 per million = USD 3,600. Total inference roughly USD 10,800 a year [ESTIMATE]. Inference is rarely the largest TCO line for a workflow like this. Integration and data engineering, the evaluation and guardrail stack, human review capacity, and ongoing model and prompt maintenance usually dominate, often by an order of magnitude.
Benchmark: arithmetic correct, states the surprise (inference is small), names what actually dominates, and notes that the pattern can reverse for high-volume conversational use cases.

**Q8. CFO challenge.** Replace year-one 40% with a benefit ramp (for example 10% in year one, 25% in year two, 40% steady state) tied to adoption milestones; convert FTE "reduction" into a mix of attrition-based reduction, redeployment and avoided hiring, each with a different cash realisation; apply a probability or haircut per benefit lever and show the P50 and P90 views; and separate hard cash benefits from capacity benefits. Present an unadjusted case, a risk-adjusted case and the break-even adoption level.
Benchmark: ramp, realisation mechanics, risk-adjustment, and a sensitivity presentation.

**Q9. Regulation.** NIST AI RMF 1.0 functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Yes: Annex III of the EU AI Act lists AI systems used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score as high-risk (excluding fraud detection). The bank (as deployer, and as provider if it built the model) faces obligations including risk management, data governance, technical documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy and robustness requirements, and registration, with the main high-risk obligations applying from August 2026 [VERIFY — the Commission's November 2025 omnibus proposal may shift dates].
Benchmark: four functions correct, Annex III recognised, deployer versus provider obligations distinguished, and a date with a verify flag.

**Q10. Positioning.** A strong answer uses the client-ask / what-we-delivered / key-decisions / client-benefit structure, states the personal decision the candidate owned (for example "I designed the six-dimension agentic-fit scoring and ran the SME calibration sessions"), and names the trade-off they argued for. The sentence an interviewer pushes on is any claim of ownership: expect "what did you personally decide, and what would have happened if you had decided differently".
Benchmark: specific personal decision, a trade-off, a quantified or qualitative client outcome, and awareness of where the probe will land.

## Module 10 — Quiz answers

**1. Four layers and postures.** Foundation model: buy via API or open-weight, never build. Platform (gateway, orchestration, vector store, evals, observability, guardrails): buy components, assemble and own the control plane. Application (agent workflows, prompts, tools, rules, integration): build for differentiating processes, buy for commodity ones. Services: partner for surge and specialist skills with capability transfer, retain product ownership. Manager-level benchmark: explains why the posture differs per layer and gives a banking example at the application layer.

**2. Golden dataset over public benchmarks.** Public benchmarks measure general capability on tasks unlike the bank's documents and decisions, so they predict domain performance poorly; and vendors optimise for public leaderboards, so scores are inflated relative to real tasks. The bank's own golden set measures the exact task, document mix and failure modes, and doubles as the baseline for ongoing monitoring and model risk validation. Manager-level benchmark: adds that the golden set also makes the evaluation reproducible when the model version changes.

**3. Weight sensitivity.** Vendor A: 4.5×0.35 + 4.0×0.15 + 4.0×0.20 + 3.0×0.15 + 3.5×0.10 + 2.5×0.05 = 1.575 + 0.60 + 0.80 + 0.45 + 0.35 + 0.125 = 3.90. Build under the same weights: 1.40 + 0.525 + 0.90 + 0.525 + 0.30 + 0.25 = 3.90. A tie, so Vendor A does not overtake; the ranking is sensitive to the exit weight and the steering committee should decide the weight explicitly. Manager-level benchmark: shows the working and interprets the tie as a governance decision rather than a scoring artefact.

**4. Five AI-specific clauses.** Data use and training prohibition (confidentiality, regulatory data rules); model change notification and version pinning (model risk validation invalidated by silent updates); quality SLAs on the client's test set (vendor accountability for accuracy, not just uptime); output IP indemnity and ownership of prompts, fine-tunes and evaluation data (infringement risk, lock-in); exit and portability with transition assistance (concentration and substitutability under outsourcing and resilience rules). Also acceptable: regulatory cooperation and audit rights; price protection. Manager-level benchmark: links each clause to a specific regulatory expectation or model risk control.

**5. Per-alert pricing with growth.** Year 1: 180,000 × 5 = EUR 0.90m. Year 2: 207,000 × 5 = EUR 1.035m. Year 3: 238,050 × 5 = EUR 1.19m. Three-year total ≈ EUR 3.13m, against EUR 2.70m at flat volume. Protections: volume-tiered pricing with declining unit rates, a cap on annual unit-price increases, a committed-volume discount in exchange for a floor, and a clause converting to a platform fee above a threshold. Manager-level benchmark: notes that per-transaction pricing transfers volume risk to the buyer and that the bank should model TCO on its own growth forecast, not the vendor's.

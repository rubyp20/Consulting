## Module 19 — Quiz answers

**1. Altitude ladder.** Task (what you physically did), workstream output, engagement recommendation, client outcome. Project B example: I ran SME interviews and mapped the underwriting process (task); the workstream produced a pain-point heatmap showing information assembly, not analysis, consumed most analyst time (output); the proposition was re-scoped from memo drafting to orchestrated assembly with the analyst owning judgement (recommendation); the client got a proposition that fits inside model-risk constraints and targets the largest cost pool (outcome). Manager-level benchmark: all four rungs in under 90 seconds, with the decision at rung three named explicitly.

**2. Reframing the "analyst work" probe.** "The scoring itself is mechanical; the judgement was in calibrating the weights with the risk function and deciding which steps were rules, which were agents and which stayed human. Those calls set the scope, and the scope set the business case." Manager-level benchmark: no defensiveness, immediate move up the ladder, names the decision owner.

Manager-level benchmark: cites the specific step and the reason (determinism, downside-only error), and connects it to the error-tolerance dimension of the agentic-fit framework in Project A.

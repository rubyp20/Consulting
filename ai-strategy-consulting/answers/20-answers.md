## Module 20 — Part A self-check quiz answers

**1. ML, GenAI, agentic.** ML learns a mapping from historical data to a prediction or label (risk: wrong predictions, drift, bias). GenAI produces content from a prompt and context (risk: wrong or ungrounded content, hallucination). Agentic AI wraps a model in a loop with tools, memory and planning to take multi-step actions (risk: wrong actions with real-world effect, compounding errors). Manager-level benchmark: "predicts, produces, acts" plus a one-line control implication for each layer.

**2. Inverted budget.** Cite BCG's 10-20-70 heuristic [PUBLIC — BCG, 2023–24]: roughly 10% algorithms, 20% technology and data, 70% people, process and change. A 60/30/10 budget is inverted and will under-deliver on adoption; propose re-balancing towards integration, evaluation, validation and change, and ask what the 60% of licences is buying at expected volumes. Manager-level benchmark: names the heuristic, states it is a heuristic not a formula, and notes that regulated banking needs a larger governance share than the rule implies.

**3. Guardrail layers.** Input (injection detection, data classification, scope filters), output (schema validation, groundedness, PII and toxicity filters, confidence routing), action (tool allow-lists, value and volume limits, idempotency and rollback, approval gates), and policy or governance (logging, versioning, kill switches, re-validation). Principle: enforce guardrails in deterministic code outside the model, because a model-based check shares the model's attack surface. Manager-level benchmark: all four layers plus the enforcement-location argument with an example such as prompt injection.

**4. Inference cost.** Input: 6 × 5,000 = 30,000 tokens × USD 3 per million = USD 0.090. Output: 6 × 500 = 3,000 tokens × USD 15 per million = USD 0.045. Per case USD 0.135; for 500,000 cases about USD 67,500 a year before caching [ESTIMATE — order of magnitude pricing, VERIFY]. Manager-level benchmark: shows the arithmetic, then observes that inference is a minor line and that caching, batching and model tiering could roughly halve it; integration and human review dominate TCO.

**5. SS1/23 principles.** Model identification and model risk classification; governance; model development, implementation and use; independent model validation; model risk mitigants [PUBLIC — PRA SS1/23, 2023; VERIFY scope]. Prompts and retrieval corpora fall under change control because they alter system behaviour as materially as a parameter change: a prompt edit or a corpus update can change outputs on the golden set, so they must be versioned, tested and re-validated like any model component. Manager-level benchmark: five principles in order, plus the "the model is the whole system" argument and the point that vendor model updates also trigger re-validation.

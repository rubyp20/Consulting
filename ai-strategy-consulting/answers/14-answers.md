## Module 14 — Quiz answers

1. **Calibrated trust, over-trust (misuse), under-trust (disuse), abuse** (Lee and See, 2004; Parasuraman and Riley, 1997). AML symptoms: calibrated, override rate tracks measured error rate and audit finds few missed escalations; misuse, analysts clear agent-prioritised alerts in seconds and blind audit finds missed suspicious activity; disuse, analysts re-run their own checks and the agent's narratives are rarely used in SAR filings; abuse, the triage agent is used to make final close decisions on alert classes it was never validated for. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer links misuse to a specific design counter-measure such as forced disagreement sampling.

2. **Analyst cost falls by roughly GBP 3.7m a year.** Before: 300,000 x 3.0 x 12 minutes = 10.8m minutes = 180,000 hours x GBP 50 = GBP 9.0m. After: 300,000 x (1.6 x 12 + 2) minutes = 300,000 x 21.2 = 6.36m minutes = 106,000 hours x GBP 50 = GBP 5.3m. Difference: 180,000 - 106,000 = 74,000 hours x GBP 50 = GBP 3.7m. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer states this is capacity released, not yet a saving, and notes the agent's run cost and the review-step quality must be netted off.

3. **Desire (and, close behind, Reinforcement).** Fear about roles suppresses Desire; unchanged performance measures (cases per hour) undermine Reinforcement. The single design choice that addresses it most directly is role redesign with changed performance measures agreed by HR, Operations and Risk before go-live, with affected staff co-designing the exception taxonomy and HITL thresholds. Manager-level benchmark: attributes ADKAR to Prosci (Hiatt, 2003) and distinguishes it from Kotter's organisational-level steps.

4. **Goodhart's law: when a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure.** If straight-through rate alone is targeted, thresholds get lowered and quality falls; pairing it with override accuracy or blind-audit error rate makes the trade-off visible. Example pairs: straight-through rate with audit error rate; handle time with rework rate; cost per case with complaints per 1,000 cases. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer says the pair should carry equal weight in the steering committee's decision rules, not just appear on the dashboard.

5. **A 6 to 12 month baseline period** (to absorb seasonality and avoid measuring an unusual month), **and a control group** (a region, product line, or randomised share of cases kept on the manual process for the first six months), plus a sustainment check at month 9 to 12 to see whether pilot-period performance persisted once observation faded. Manager-level benchmark: mentions pre-agreed attribution rules with Finance for KPIs that other initiatives also move.

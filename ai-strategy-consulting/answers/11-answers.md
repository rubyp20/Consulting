## Module 11 — Quiz answers

**1. Stage questions.** PoC: can the technique do the task at acceptable quality on our data? Pilot: does it work in the real workflow with real users, and what is the measured benefit? MVP: can it run in production with controls at partial volume and hold its KPIs? Scale: does the benefit hold across the full population and can the organisation run it? A PoC cannot prove business value because it runs on a sample outside the workflow, so it measures neither user behaviour change nor benefit against a baseline. Manager-level benchmark: adds that booking PoC results in a business case is the most common source of later credibility loss.

**2. Four dimensions with pilot examples.** Technical: accuracy on live data within one percentage point of golden-set accuracy. Operational: handle time per case falls to a stated target and HITL override rate sits within a defined band. Business: benefit measured on the pilot population and extrapolated with a haircut, signed by the business owner. Risk: pilot risk assessment closed, fairness check passed, zero severity-1 incidents. Manager-level benchmark: names who owns each threshold, particularly that risk and accuracy thresholds belong to the second line.

**3. Hours and FTE.** Auto-closed: 147,000 × 0.44 = 64,680 alerts × 9 min = 582,120 min ≈ 9,700 hours. Remaining: 82,320 alerts × 3.3 min = 271,656 min ≈ 4,530 hours. Total ≈ 14,230 hours; at 1,600 productive hours ≈ 8.9 FTE equivalents. Manager-level benchmark: distinguishes FTE equivalents (capacity) from booked FTE (headcount bridge).

**4. Low override rate.** A very low override rate can indicate automation bias: analysts accepting proposals without genuine review, which removes the human control the model risk tier depends on. It can also indicate that analysts are not using the tool at all if measured on the wrong denominator. Set a band with a floor and a ceiling and test override quality through QA sampling. Manager-level benchmark: proposes a specific control such as seeded test cases or randomised blind review to detect automation bias.

**5. Six causes and remedies.** No production path (define architecture, integration and funding in principle before PoC); no business owner (named owner signs hypothesis and criteria); data access not secured (start approvals in parallel with PoC); model risk engaged late (classify and agree evidence pack at PoC start); vague or moving criteria (quantified four-dimension gates with stopping rule); benefit not measured (pilot in workflow with control or before/after); technology before problem (use-case assessment first); run costs and team ignored (TCO and operating model at pilot exit); no change capacity (fund change in MVP); point solutions (platform after second PoC). Any six with correct pairing. Manager-level benchmark: sequences the remedies and names the first three to act on.

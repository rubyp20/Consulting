## Module 17 — Quiz answers

1. **Klarna 2024 claims sit at rung 1 to 2; HSBC AML AI at rung 3.** Klarna's two-thirds-of-chats figure was a first-month production metric (rung 2) but the USD 40m profit figure was a forecast (rung 1); the 2025 correction confirms the haircut was warranted. HSBC's results came from a system in live use across markets with regulator-visible metrics (rung 3), though no booked P&L figure has been disclosed, so it is not rung 4. Manager-level benchmark: distinguishes the volume claim from the financial claim within the same case, and says explicitly which rung each sits on and why.

2. **Commonwealth Bank of Australia (2025) is the baseline-error case.** Capacity was removed on a forecast reduction in call volumes that did not materialise. The sequencing rule: release capacity only after deflection is observed in production telemetry for at least one full demand cycle, and book redeployment before headcount release in the business case. Manager-level benchmark: cites the case, names the driver, and states the rule as a design principle rather than a lesson learned.

3. **Correction: Erica is predominantly intent classification and rules-based fulfilment, not generative AI, and its success rests on a bounded scope with deterministic actions.** The evidence it provides is that bounded, deterministic customer automation works at scale; it says little about open-ended GenAI chat in front of customers, where the relevant public evidence is Klarna's correction and Air Canada's liability ruling. Manager-level benchmark: corrects the pattern mislabel politely and immediately offers the cases that do bear on the client's question.

4. **200,000 employees is an access or licence count: it measures deployment, not value.** Outcome metrics to track instead: weekly active usage by role (a leading indicator) and a value metric tied to a specific workflow, such as hours released per analyst in a defined process or cycle-time reduction in a named use case that rides on the platform. Manager-level benchmark: distinguishes input, adoption and outcome metrics, and ties value to vertical use cases rather than the enabler.

5. **Arithmetic: 240,000 alerts × 45 minutes = 180,000 hours = 112.5 FTE at 1,600 hours.** A 60% alert reduction removes 144,000 alerts, 108,000 hours, 67.5 FTE of capacity. First-year realised saving will be lower because the remaining alerts are richer and take longer to investigate, because validators and regulators need to accept the model before thresholds are retired, and because capacity converts to cost only through attrition or redeployment. Manager-level benchmark: shows the arithmetic cleanly, labels it an estimate, and names at least two reasons for the first-year gap without being asked.

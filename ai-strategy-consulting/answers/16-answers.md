## Module 16 — Quiz answers

1. **Real-time authorisation fraud scoring: classical or deep-learning ML**, because decisions are needed in tens of milliseconds with deterministic, explainable, monitored behaviour; LLMs are excluded from the real-time path. **SAR narrative drafting: GenAI (RAG-grounded)**, because it is unstructured text generation from an evidence pack, with mandatory analyst sign-off. **Applying chargeback time limits: rules engine**, because scheme rules are deterministic, change on a schedule and need a named owner and audit trail. **Investigating a non-receipt claim: agentic orchestration**, because it is multi-step across systems and correspondents with unstructured messages and judgement at branches, with fund movements kept behind deterministic controls. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer explains that most processes contain all four kinds of work and the skill is in the decomposition.

2. **Net annual benefit of roughly GBP 4.5m.** Current cost: 1.5m x GBP 8 = GBP 12.0m. Effort reduction: 45% x 12.0m = GBP 5.4m capacity released. Run cost: 1.5m x GBP 0.60 = GBP 0.9m. Net: 5.4 - 0.9 = GBP 4.5m. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer labels the GBP 5.4m as capacity released rather than saving, notes build cost and validation effort are excluded, and adds the non-cost benefits (SLA breaches, liquidity) qualitatively.

3. **Credit underwriting.** Annex III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 lists AI systems used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural persons or establish their credit score as high-risk; the distinction that limits the effect is that corporate and SME lending to legal persons falls outside that annex entry, though it remains subject to model-risk governance (SS1/23, SR 11-7) and anti-discrimination law. Both points `[VERIFY]` given the proposed digital omnibus changes to timelines. Manager-level benchmark: also notes that preparation steps (spreading, memo drafting) carry lower regulatory weight than the decision itself.

4. **The test: compare the cost of a false positive versus a false negative at the decision point, and ask whether the action is reversible.** Releasing a payment after a sanctions hit: a false negative (releasing a true match) is irreversible and a regulatory breach, so no autonomy above a conservative threshold and human decision with full logging. Issuing a GBP 20 provisional credit: the error is bounded and reversible (the credit can be reversed after review), so high autonomy with sampling review is appropriate. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer frames the output as an autonomy level by decision class that can be presented to a regulator.

5. **Because structured fields replace free text.** ISO 20022 carries structured names and addresses, LEIs, purpose codes and the end-to-end UETR, which reduce false-positive hits at screening, give the resolution agent reliable identifiers to compare, and let an investigation agent locate and trace a payment across correspondents without parsing MT-style narrative fields. The effect is higher extraction accuracy, fewer ambiguous cases and more reliable tool calls. Manager-level benchmark: a strong answer notes that the benefit depends on counterparties actually populating the structured fields, which is uneven during the coexistence period `[VERIFY]`.

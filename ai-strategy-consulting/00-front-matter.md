# How to use this document

This document replaces the interactive, one-module-at-a-time format with a complete self-study programme. It covers twenty modules across five phases, four one-page recaps, and an answer-key appendix.

## Reading protocol

1. **Start with Module 0.** Attempt the ten-question diagnostic without looking at the answer key. Score yourself using the rubric. The band tells you whether to read every module in order or to move quickly through Phase 1.
2. **Read one module at a sitting.** Each module follows the same nine-part structure: why a client cares, concept and mechanism, frameworks, worked BFSI example, real-world case, consultant language, common mistakes, interview questions with model answers, and a five-question self-check quiz.
3. **Attempt the quiz before opening the appendix.** Quiz answers are deliberately placed in the appendix at the back, not in the module, so that you test recall rather than recognition. Each answer carries a short explanation and a note on what a manager-level AI consultant would add.
4. **Revise from the recaps.** After Modules 5, 10, 15 and 20 there is a one-page recap built for revision the night before an interview.
5. **Finish with Module 20.** Work the forty questions, the five case drills and the three guesstimates, then sit the mock interview and grade yourself with the rubrics provided.

## Labelling conventions used throughout

Every example, number, client and case carries one of these labels at first mention. Treat them as a contract with you about what is real.

| Label | Meaning | How to use it in an interview |
|---|---|---|
| [PUBLIC — source, year] | Publicly reported fact, with the organisation or publication and year | Cite it, with the source and year, and say if figures may have moved |
| [ILLUSTRATIVE — composite] | A realistic but invented engagement, client or number | Use it as a pattern, never as a client reference |
| [ESTIMATE — assumption] | The author's own assumption, with the assumption stated | Say "my working assumption is" and be ready to flex it |
| [VERIFY — date-sensitive] | Regulation status, model pricing, vendor capability or fast-moving statistic | Check it the week before any interview or client meeting |

## A note on frameworks and attribution

Each framework is attributed to its originator where this is known. Where a framework is generic practice with no clear originator (for example a value-versus-feasibility two-by-two or a weighted scoring model), it is described as "generic, widely used". Where attribution is uncertain, this is stated rather than guessed. In an interview, attributing a framework to the wrong firm is a credibility error that a partner will notice.

## What was added to the curriculum and why

Three additions were made to the twenty-module curriculum as originally specified. Each is small and sits inside an existing module rather than adding a new one.

- **Evaluation design as a first-class topic (Module 4 and Module 11).** Interviewers in 2025–26 increasingly probe how you would know an agent is working. Golden datasets, LLM-as-judge, and numeric gate thresholds are now expected vocabulary, not specialist knowledge.
- **Model-risk classification of GenAI and agentic systems (Module 12).** Banks are being asked by supervisors how LLM-based systems fit into frameworks written for statistical models (SR 11-7, PRA SS1/23). Being able to tier an agentic system under a bank's existing model risk policy is a differentiator.
- **The public reversals (Module 17).** Klarna's re-hiring of humans and Commonwealth Bank of Australia's reversal of AI-linked redundancies are now standard interview material. A strategy consultant must be able to explain why value claims were walked back, not just what was announced.

# Recap 4 — Modules 16 to 20

Revise this the night before an interview. Drawn from Modules 16 to 20; module numbers are in brackets.

## Ten things to remember

1. Every BFSI process has four kinds of work: rules, structured-data ML, unstructured GenAI, and multi-step orchestration with judgement (agentic with HITL); decompose first, then decide whether an agent is the right container. [16]
2. Risk asymmetry sets autonomy: irreversible with catastrophic false negatives means human; bounded and reversible means straight-through; the rest gets sampled or threshold review. [16]
3. Scheme rules and credit policy live in a rules engine with an owner, not a prompt; GenAI is not the fraud or screening engine; ISO 20022 structured data is the payments-operations enabler. [16]
4. The biggest number is not built first: wave one proves the pattern and builds the platform so the largest value lands in year two with lower risk. [16]
5. Place every case on the claimed-versus-realised ladder (press release, pilot metric, production telemetry, audited P&L) with its haircut before quoting it. [17]
6. Five walk-back drivers: baseline error (CBA), quality-cost mispriced (Klarna), liability not designed in (Air Canada), competence envelope (Zillow), explainability not evidenced (Apple Card); quote outcome and caveat in one sentence. [17]
7. Engagement walkthrough in six beats: ask and shape, diagnostic as a number, hypothesis and whether it held, one analysis in depth, recommendation and decision, what you would do differently; make your role explicit. [18]
8. Reuse one shared assumption set (UK analyst GBP 62,000; EU EUR 80,000; US card ops USD 52,000; India USD 22,000; 1,600 hours; 10% discount; frontier USD 3 and 15 per million tokens) and say so. [18]
9. State every project at four rungs (task, workstream, engagement, client); answer strategy-versus-operations by moving up the ladder in the same sentence; the prototype retired design uncertainties; own the question, not every answer. [19]
10. Mock score out of 30: under 15 foundations, 15 to 21 practitioner, 22 to 26 manager, 27 to 30 ready; guesstimates need a structure, labelled assumptions and a sanity check against a known total. [20]

## Phrases to use with a client

> "The autonomy level follows the risk asymmetry at each decision: irreversible and asymmetric means human; bounded and reversible means straight-through." [16]

> "Let me separate what was claimed, what was measured, and what has since been corrected." [17]

> "At your volumes, an HSBC-type alert reduction translates to roughly this much analyst capacity; let me show you the arithmetic and the assumptions." [17]

> "I built the prototype to retire the three design uncertainties that would otherwise have been argued on slides." [19]

> "Deterministic steps belong in rules; judgement steps belong with the agent; consequential decisions stay with a human. The six dimensions are how we drew those lines." [19]

## Traps

- Pitching GenAI as the fraud or screening engine, embedding scheme rules in prompts, or treating all eleven library use cases as "agentic". [16]
- Quoting the headline as the outcome ("Klarna saved 700 FTE"), calling HSBC AML AI or Erica GenAI, or using Zillow and Air Canada without a banking bridge. [17]
- Walking through an engagement as a list of activities with no number in the diagnostic and no analysis you can defend arithmetically. [18]
- Activity lists instead of decisions, claiming the team's result as personal delivery, numbers without labels, and no reflective point. [19]
- Answering a guesstimate with a number before a structure, or quoting the Gartner, MIT and RAND statistics without their caveats. [20]

## Thirty-second self-test

1. Name the four kinds of work and the AI pattern for each.
2. State the four rungs of the claimed-versus-realised ladder with typical haircuts.
3. Give the six beats of an engagement walkthrough and the "what I would do differently" for one simulation.
4. Deliver your KYB project at all four rungs of the altitude ladder in four sentences.
5. Build the structure for the relationship-manager inference-cost guesstimate without numbers.

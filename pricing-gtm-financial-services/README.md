# Pricing & Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy in Financial Services

### A practitioner's guide in the style of an MBB FS practice: Cards · Lending · Real-Time Payments · Cross-Border

This guide explains what "pricing strategy" and "GTM strategy" work actually involves when a strategy firm does it for banks, card issuers, payment networks, acquirers, and fintechs. "Strategy firm" here means McKinsey, BCG, or Bain ("MBB"), or a specialist such as Simon-Kucher, Oliver Wyman, or the Big-4 strategy arms. The aim is that you can decode job descriptions, speak credibly in interviews, and be useful from day one on an engagement.

## The one-page mental model

**Pricing in FS** means choosing the **price metric** (what you charge for), the **structure** (tiers, bundles, fences), the **level** (how much), and the **governance** (who can deviate, and how you stop leakage). You do this knowing that in FS, price moves four things at once:

1. **Volume**: the classic demand effect.
2. **Risk mix**: a higher rate attracts riskier borrowers. This is adverse selection.
3. **Conduct and regulatory outcomes**: fair lending, fair value, fee caps, transparency.
4. **Relationship value**: a loss-leader loan can win a treasury mandate, and "free" checking is paid for by deposit margin.

**GTM** is the integrated answer to five questions:

1. **Where to play:** which segments, geographies, use cases, and corridors?
2. **How to win:** what value proposition, and what is our right to win?
3. **How to reach:** which channels, partners, and sales motion?
4. **How to monetise:** what pricing and packaging?
5. **How to run it:** what launch sequence, operating model, economics, and KPIs?

### The four product areas have different "money engines"

| | **Cards** | **Lending** | **Real-Time Payments** | **Cross-Border** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Main revenue engine** | Interest + interchange + fees − rewards − credit losses | Spread over funding cost (NIM) − credit losses + fees | Per-transaction fees, value-added services, and indirect value (primacy, deposits) | FX spread + explicit fees + value-added FX (hedging) |
| **Main pricing levers** | APR tiers, annual fee, rewards earn/burn, promos; MDR for merchants | Rate grids by risk × term × channel, fees, relationship pricing | Tiered per-transaction fees, packaging, Request-for-Pay, VAS | FX margin by corridor × ticket size × speed, flat fees, subscriptions |
| **Binding constraints** | Interchange regulation, CARD Act, rewards arms race | Fair lending, adverse selection, capital | Expectation that P2P is free, EU price-parity rule, fraud (APP) | Transparency rules, compliance cost, liquidity, correspondent de-risking |
| **Dominant GTM plays** | Co-brand partnerships, digital direct, cross-sell, embedded commercial cards | Digital direct, intermediaries (brokers, dealers), embedded/POS, RM-led SME and corporate | Use-case- and vertical-led, receive-first network building, B2B2X via fintechs, Pay-by-Bank | Corridor-led, D2C diaspora digital, B2B2X platforms (white-label), RM-led corporate FX |

## Where these roles sit (to decode the JDs you're seeing)

| Employer type | Examples | What "pricing strategy" means there |
|---|---|---|
| **MBB / strategy firms** | McKinsey, BCG, Bain | Pricing is one lever inside larger growth, turnaround, or commercial-excellence programmes. You build diagnostics, redesign pricing, and set up governance, usually at C-suite level. |
| **Pricing specialists** | Simon-Kucher | Pricing *is* the product. This means deep WTP research (conjoint), price architecture, and implementation. |
| **FS-specialist strategy firms** | Oliver Wyman, Accenture Strategy / Capco, Big-4 strategy arms (EY-Parthenon, Strategy&, Deloitte Monitor, KPMG Strategy) | Heavier on risk-based pricing, analytics builds, and implementation and tooling |
| **In-house** | Card issuers, networks (Visa, Mastercard), acquirers, banks (payments and treasury), fintechs (Wise, Stripe, Adyen, Revolut) | Owning the P&L levers: rate cards, merchant pricing, deal desks, and launch pricing. Less slide-making, more "live" decisions |
| **Due diligence (CDD)** | MBB, EY-Parthenon, L.E.K., PE value-creation teams | Stress-testing a target's pricing power and GTM scalability for an investment thesis |

A phrase-by-phrase JD decoder will be in chapter 08 (in progress).

## Conventions used in this guide

- **Illustrative** numbers are realistic orders of magnitude to teach the mechanics. They are not client data or market facts, so re-derive them from sources before using them in any client or interview setting.
- **"Verify"** flags mark regulatory and market items that change often. Read them as "correct to the best of our knowledge at time of writing (September 2026); check the current status."
- **Examples are composites.** Engagement stories are anonymised composites of typical situations. They are not descriptions of any real client.
- The spelling mixes UK and US conventions because FS consulting does. APR, NIM, and similar terms will be defined in chapter 09 (in progress).

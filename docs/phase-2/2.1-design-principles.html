1. Why principles come before maps

A to-be map is a sequence of decisions: keep this step, remove that one, move this check earlier, automate that one. Without agreed principles, each decision is argued on its own and the loudest view wins; the result is the current process with fewer boxes. With principles signed first, each decision is tested against a rule the Design Authority has already accepted, and only the genuinely new questions reach the Authority.

The ten principles below were not invented for this phase. Each is the design response to one of the six underlying causes established in Phase 1, or to a constraint the RFP imposed. The table shows the lineage.

How the principles are used

  • Every design decision in 2.3, 2.4 and 2.8 cites the principle(s) it applies. A decision that cites none is returned.
  • A decision that conflicts with a principle is escalated to the Design Authority with the conflict stated; the Authority may grant an exception, recorded with reason and expiry.
  • Where two principles conflict, the precedence in section 3 applies.
  • The principles bind the Replacement Programme’s configuration of these journeys through the requirements pack (2.8), which cites them.

2. The principles

DP-01 · Route by risk and change materiality, not by deal typeU1 · levers L1, L5, L12
StatementThe path a deal follows is determined by its risk attributes and the materiality of what is changing, evaluated from system-held data. Deal type is a parameter that sets rules within a pattern; it does not define a workflow.
PermitsFast-track routes for low-risk unchanged renewals and administrative variations; exception-only Credit involvement for pre-approved drawdowns; one configured decision flow with parameters.
ForbidsA new workflow per deal type; routing that cannot be explained by a stated rule; “if required” steps with no criterion.
TestCan this step’s inclusion or exclusion for a given deal be stated as a rule on data the system holds at that point? If not, either capture the data or the step applies to all.
ArbiterHead of Credit Risk Policy on routing criteria; Design Authority on pattern structure.
Known tensionWith DP-03: a fast-track that bypasses a Credit assessment must show equivalence (2.5) or a policy decision extending authority. With DP-10: the platform must support rule-based routing natively or the routing goes on the custom list.
DP-02 · Every control is a rule the system applies and recordsU2 · levers L3, L9
StatementEach control in the inventory (1.6) is expressed as a rule with defined inputs, a defined outcome and recorded evidence (what was checked, against what, by whom or what, when). Judgement is retained where policy requires it, but the fact and basis of the judgement are recorded.
PermitsAutomated delegated-authority determination with recorded authority level and rule version; rules tables per pattern × risk band for required analytics and documents; manual override with reason.
ForbidsControls that exist only as practice; checks performed on data the system holds without the system recording the check; approvals without a recorded authority level.
TestFor this control: what are the inputs, what is the rule, what evidence is written, and where is the policy reference? Any blank fails.
ArbiterHead of Credit Risk Policy (rule content); second-line Risk (evidence sufficiency).
Known tensionWith DP-10: rules engines vary in depth; rules the platform cannot express natively are custom (2.7) or remain manual with recorded evidence.
DP-03 · No control is removed or automated without equivalenceRFP §7 · 1.6 · deliverable 2.5
StatementA to-be map is not approved while it eliminates or automates a control without a corresponding entry in the control equivalence register (2.5) showing the replacement control, the evidence it produces, the residual risk, and the second-line decision. Regulatory controls (C15, C17, C27 and the KYC element of C1) are retained in every option; only their mechanism may change.
PermitsAutomation that strengthens a control (system check replacing manual); relocation of a control earlier in the flow; consolidation of duplicate checks.
ForbidsRemoval justified by speed alone; “we never use it” as evidence; changes to regulatory capture or timing without parallel-run reconciliation.
TestIs there a signed register entry for this change? If the answer is a policy question rather than an equivalence question, route to Credit Risk Policy and retain the as-is control until answered.
ArbiterSecond-line Risk (equivalence); Head of Credit Risk Policy (policy questions).
Known tensionWith DP-01 and DP-07: the fast-track and the graduated staleness control are the two largest equivalence cases; they are opened first.
DP-04 · Every wait has an owner, a timer and a taskU3 · levers L5, L7
StatementWherever a deal stops moving, the system knows who it is waiting on, how long it has waited, what the expected duration is, and what happens when that is exceeded. Hand-offs between teams create a task in the receiver’s queue, visible to the sender.
PermitsQueues per team with SLAs; timers and escalations; customer-side waits tracked (letters, information requests); queue position visible to the RM.
ForbidsHand-offs by message, email or report; stages that end at “ready” with no receiver task; open-ended waits; batch jobs standing in for timers.
TestAt this point, if nothing happens for ten working days, who is notified and what do they see?
ArbiterHead of Lending Operations (SLAs and ownership); Design Authority.
Known tensionTeam ownership of SLAs is an operating-model decision (2.9), not a configuration decision; the design must not set SLAs nobody has agreed to own.
DP-05 · Triggers are system events that open work in contextU3 · lever L2
StatementWork begins from a system event (review date, customer request logged, early-warning flag, referral) that opens the deal with the customer, parties, existing facilities and balances already loaded. The deal start is one shared component with two entry variants (new customer; existing relationship).
PermitsRenewal tasks generated from review dates; landing page as a work queue; single sign-on; lead capture that becomes a deal without re-keying.
ForbidsReports as the trigger for routine work; repeating the login–search–locate–retrieve sequence per deal; an opportunity record that adds a step without adding information.
TestWhen this journey starts, what does the user already see without searching?
ArbiterDesign Authority; Solution Architect on platform capability.
DP-06 · Documents and requests are generated, delivered, received and evidenced on the platformU4 · levers L4, L8, L11, L15
StatementFacility letters, security instructions, strategy memos and information requests are produced from deal data, delivered through the platform, returned or answered through it, and their status is a system fact. Terms exist in the system before they exist in a document.
PermitsTemplate-driven letters per pattern and product incl. tranches and shadow limits; e-delivery and e-signature with a wet-ink exception path; structured information requests; security instruction generated from approval conditions.
ForbidsOff-platform letters or memos; manual issue by email; document checks that cannot reconcile to system terms; requests to customers tracked in mailboxes.
TestCan the system state, for this document, when it was issued, to whom, whether it was returned, and that its content matches the approved deal?
ArbiterHead of Lending Operations; Solution Architect (capability); Lending Services (security documents).
Known tensionWith DP-10: if the platform lacks e-signature natively, this principle justifies a custom or partner integration; the Design Authority decides whether the value (OP-15) warrants it.
DP-07 · Rework paths are designed, bounded and measuredU4 · levers L5, L11
StatementDeferral, decline, appeal, post-approval change and staleness are first-class paths with defined entry criteria, a maximum number of iterations or elapsed time, an escalation, and a measured rate. Prevention precedes handling: the submission standard is enforced before submission so that rework is the exception.
PermitsStructured deferral with at most two loops before escalation; graduated staleness control replacing the hard reset; materiality rules for post-approval change; decline reason codes.
ForbidsLoop-back arrows with no limit; full re-approval for non-material change or timing failure; declines without a recorded reason.
TestFor this loop: what is the maximum, what happens at the maximum, and which metric in 1.4 reports it?
ArbiterHead of Credit (Credit-side loops); Head of Lending Operations.
DP-08 · Distress is surfaced by data before effort is spentU5 · lever L10
StatementEarly-warning indicators the system already holds (arrears, grade migration, covenant breach, forbearance) are combined into a flag shown at deal creation and on the work queue, so that referral is prompted before analysis and assessment effort, and so that distressed connections are excluded from fast-track routes. A referral is a record on the connection, not a transfer of a file.
PermitsReferral prompts; exclusion criteria for DP-01 routes; parallel Credit and SBS review where CAP policy allows.
ForbidsA distress screen that sits only after full assessment; referral dependent solely on RM judgement; sequential re-reading of the same memo by two authorities without a policy reason.
TestWould the system have flagged this connection before the RM opened the deal?
ArbiterHead of SBS; Head of Credit Risk Policy (CAP policy).
DP-09 · Capture once, at source, with a named owner; protected fields are a constraintU6 · RFP §7 · levers L14, L15
StatementEvery data element has one capture point, a structured form, and a named business owner. The protected-fields list (1.8 §6) is a constraint on every design decision: a lever that touches a P1 field must show where and when the field is captured in the to-be and must plan a parallel-run reconciliation at cut-over. Measurement (stage timestamps, task time) is designed in, not surveyed afterwards.
PermitsPull-through from CIF and product systems; structured memos; reason codes; old-to-new deal-type mapping for history.
ForbidsRe-keying between systems; free text where a code is reportable; removing or re-timing a reporting field without the owner’s sign-off; designs whose benefit cannot be measured from the platform.
TestFor every field this step creates or changes: who owns it, which report uses it, and is it captured here or earlier?
ArbiterHead of MI & Regulatory Reporting; named field owners.
DP-10 · Standard before custom; configure before buildRFP §7 · lever L13 · deliverable 2.7
StatementWhere the replacement platform provides a standard lending process or capability, the design adopts it unless a documented business, regulatory or control reason requires deviation. Each to-be step is classified Fit, Configure, Custom or Gap (2.7); the custom list is short, justified and approved as a whole by the Design Authority.
PermitsAdapting a to-be map to the platform’s standard sequence where the outcome is the same; retiring workarounds in favour of native capability; parameterising rather than customising.
ForbidsRebuilding a workaround because it is familiar; custom build to preserve a step DP-01 to DP-09 would remove; “as-is on the new system” as a fallback.
TestDoes the platform do this as standard? If yes, why not use it? If no, which principle requires it?
ArbiterReplacement Solution Architect (what is standard); Design Authority (whether deviation is justified).
Known tensionWith DP-06 and DP-01 where the platform is weaker than the design requires. The Design Authority resolves by value: custom is justified where an opportunity in 1.10 of size L or XL depends on it.

3. Precedence, exceptions and the decision record

3.1 Precedence when principles conflict

  1. DP-03 and DP-09 are constraints. They are never traded off. A design that cannot satisfy them is changed, not excepted.
  2. DP-01, DP-02, DP-04, DP-06, DP-07, DP-08 define the target. Where two conflict, the one that addresses the higher-scoring underlying cause (1.9 §6) takes precedence, and the conflict is recorded.
  3. DP-05 and DP-10 govern how the target is reached. They yield to the target principles where the Design Authority judges the value justifies it, by reference to 1.10.

3.2 Exceptions

An exception is granted only by the Design Authority, in a sitting, recorded in the decision log with: the principle excepted, the decision it applies to, the reason, the residual consequence, and an expiry or review point. Exceptions are reported in the G2 pack. An exception to DP-03 or DP-09 is not available.

3.4 Worked example

DDR-001Renewals meeting the eligibility rule are approved within line authority without transfer to Credit (P2 fast-track route)
PrinciplesDP-01 (route by risk); DP-02 (eligibility as a recorded rule); DP-08 (early-warning flag as exclusion). In tension with DP-03: Credit assessment (C6) is bypassed for eligible deals → equivalence entry CE-001 (automated DA determination C5 with recorded authority; eligibility rule; post-approval sampling by Credit) and policy question PQ-001 (extension of renewal authority) routed to Credit Risk Policy. Route is implemented only on PQ-001 answered.
Options(a) retain full Credit route for all renewals; (b) fast-track within existing authority if policy already permits; (c) fast-track with extended authority; (d) tiered: (b) now, (c) on policy decision. Chosen: (d).
EvidencePP-09 (score 25); A1 confirmed; A2 cohort share and outcomes (1.4 step 8); SR/SD precedent.
DataF04 limits and dates, F06 risk grade (P1): grade refresh rule required on fast-track; F07 authority level captured.
Fit-gapConfigure if platform routing supports rule evaluation on grade, limit delta, covenant status, flags; else Custom (rules engine).

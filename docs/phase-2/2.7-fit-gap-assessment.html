1. Purpose and the principle it serves

DP-10 says: standard before custom; configure before build. This assessment is how that principle is applied. Each requirement in the pack is tested against what the platform does as standard, and the result decides one of four things: adopt the standard (Fit), set it up (Configure), build or integrate (Custom), or accept that the capability does not exist (Gap) and apply the fallback. The output is a short, justified custom list that the Design Authority approves as a whole, and a view of build effort for 2.12 and the business case.

Two failure modes are guarded against. The first is customising the platform to reproduce the as-is; the assessment requires every Custom item to cite the design principle that needs it and the opportunity in 1.10 whose value depends on it. The second is designing against a platform that is weaker than assumed; the assessment states, for each capability, what the design does if the platform cannot provide it, so that no Release 1 outcome rests on an unconfirmed capability.

1.2 Decision rules

  1. Fit over Configure: if the platform’s standard sequence achieves the same outcome, controls and evidence as the to-be map, the map is adjusted to the platform, not the reverse. The DDR records the adjustment.
  2. Configure over Custom: a capability achievable through configuration is never built, even if configuration is less elegant.
  3. Custom only with a principle and a value: a Custom item must cite the DP-nn that requires it and the OP-nn (size L or XL) that depends on it, or a regulatory / control requirement (2.5). Otherwise it is a Gap with fallback.
  4. Gap means fallback is live at go-live: no Gap is accepted without its 2.8 fallback being operable (NFR-07).
  5. Custom list approved as a whole: the Design Authority sees the full list with total effort, not item by item, so the cumulative cost of deviation is visible (DP-10).
  6. Protected fields are not negotiable: any Gap touching a P1 field (1.8 §6) is escalated; the design does not accept it.

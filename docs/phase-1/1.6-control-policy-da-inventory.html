1. Purpose and why it matters more than the maps

In a bank, a process step survives a redesign only if the control it serves survives. The maps say what is done; this inventory says why it is done, who requires it, and what would have to be true for it to be done differently. It is the document the Design Authority and second line use in Phase 2 to approve or refuse each change, through the control equivalence register (2.5). A step with no entry here cannot be eliminated or automated in the to-be design.

What this means for Phase 2

  • The fast-track question is a policy-application question. Whether low-risk renewals can be approved within RM/line authority depends on what the DA matrix already allows (section 3, row 3). If it already allows it, L1 is a process change and the equivalence case is C5 automation. If not, L1 needs Credit Risk Policy to extend authority, which is a policy decision outside this project’s remit and must be raised at G1.
  • No regulatory control is removed in any lever. C15, C17, C27 are retained and protected; the levers change how they are performed.
  • Six controls get stronger. The equivalence register should lead with these; they establish credibility with second line before the harder cases.
  • The deferral loop is a C3 failure. If validation caught what Credit needs, fewer deals would defer. System validation rules are the equivalence for a manual check that does not work.

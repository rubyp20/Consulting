1. Purpose and principles

This is the document the business case is measured against. Every KPI target in Phase 2 and every benefit tracked in Phase 4 refers back to a number defined and measured here. Four principles govern it:

  1. Definitions before data. Each metric has a precise start event, end event, population and exclusion rule, agreed with MI before the extract is analysed, so that the same definition can be re-measured on the replacement platform.
  2. Distributions, not averages. Means hide the tail, and the tail is where rework lives. Every time metric is reported at P50, P80 and P95 with the count.
  3. Ranges where sources disagree. Where DR-02 coverage is partial or survey and timestamps diverge, the baseline is a range. The SteerCo endorses the range.
  4. Restated volume base. ESR Cleanse is excluded (SD-01, 1.3 §5.1). All percentages use live customer deals (26,745) unless stated.

What the baseline is not

It is not an effort measure (that is 1.5, which feeds the touch-time and cost lines here), not a control assessment (1.6), and not a root-cause analysis (1.9). It is the quantified description of how long things take, how often they go wrong, and what they cost.

Clock rules

  • Calendar days, not working days: the customer experiences calendar time. Working-day variants reported for Credit queue (S2a) and Fulfilment (S5) where SLAs are internal.
  • Loops do not stop the clock. A deferred deal’s S2 runs from first submission to final decision; the loop is counted separately (section 3, R-metrics).
  • A deal reset at the 3-month letter point is one deal with a long S3b, not two deals. Re-originated deals (W-01 batch) are linked to the original by customer and product where DR-01 allows, otherwise flagged.
  • Withdrawn and declined deals are included in volume and in S1/S2 timing; excluded from S3–S5.

What the illustration shows about the method: if the measured renewal profile is anything like this, three facts would drive the design: wait is more than 90% of elapsed time, the documentation stage is half of it, and a quarter of Credit-routed renewals loop at least once. The levers would then be letter automation (L4), fast-track routing (L1) and submission validation (L11), in that order of cycle-time impact. The measured run in Week 7 will confirm, re-order, or overturn that.

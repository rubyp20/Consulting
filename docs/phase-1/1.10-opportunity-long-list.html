1. Method

1.1 From root cause to opportunity

Opportunities are generated from the six underlying causes in 1.9 §6, not from the pain points directly. This keeps the list short and structural: an opportunity that removes a cause removes every pain point the cause drives. Each cause was worked through ECRS-A in order, because the order matters: eliminating a step is worth more than automating it, and automating a step that should have been eliminated is the classic failure of system replacements.

StepQuestion asked of each causeTypical result
E EliminateCan the work be stopped altogether, because the control it served is unnecessary or served elsewhere?Retire ESR Cleanse deals; retire dead deal types; stop re-instructing unchanged security
C CombineCan steps, hand-offs or views be merged so the work happens once?Context-aware deal start; single completion view
R RearrangeCan the sequence or routing change so effort is proportionate to risk and decisions happen earlier?Fast-track renewals; materiality triage; early-warning flags; graduated staleness control
S SimplifyCan the rule, the structure or the artefact be made simpler and explicit?Five patterns; rules tables; structured requests; reason codes
A AutomateOnly now: can what remains be done by the system?DA determination; letters and e-signature; tasks and timers; integrations

1.3 Sizing rules

  • Capacity is sized as hours saved ÷ 1,600 productive hours (placeholder) = FTE-equivalent, by role. It is capacity, not headcount (SD-04; 0.9 counting rule 2).
  • Cycle time is sized as days removed from the P50 of a stage on the affected population, from the 1.4 stage model.
  • Where two opportunities act on the same step, the saving is attributed once (to the one listed first) and the second is marked “sized within”. Totals in §4 are therefore not double-counted.
  • Risk, control, MI and experience value is described, not monetised, at this stage (0.9 counting rule 4).
  • Dis-benefits are listed in §6 and carried into the business case.

5.1 Size versus ease

Numbers are OP-IDs. Colour is the dependency. Position uses the illustrative size band; at v1.0 items move as measured inputs replace assumptions.

5.2 Reading

  • Top-right (large and easy): OP-06 automated DA, OP-10 renewal tasks, OP-14 context-aware deal start, OP-17 submission standard, OP-18 structured requests, OP-16 graduated staleness (policy). These are configuration on the replacement with strong control arguments. They should be the first design workshops in Phase 2.
  • Top-left (large and hard): OP-01 fast-track (policy), OP-15 letters (capability), OP-02 triage, OP-11 queues and SLAs. The value concentrates here. Each needs a decision owner identified at G1: Credit Risk Policy for OP-01; Solution Architect for OP-15 (fit-gap); Credit for OP-02 criteria.
  • Bottom-right (small and easy): OP-04, OP-29, OP-30, OP-31, OP-08, OP-20, OP-24, OP-26, OP-28. Do them; several are quick wins before the platform changes.
  • Bottom-left: OP-19, OP-32, OP-33 integration items: Programme scope; this project supplies requirements, not business case.

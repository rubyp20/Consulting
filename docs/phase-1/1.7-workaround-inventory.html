1. Purpose and the rule this document enforces

A workaround is anything that exists because the process or the system could not do what was needed: a bot that re-keys, a batch job that applies a rule the workflow should hold, a letter produced in Word because the platform cannot generate it, a spreadsheet that tracks what the system does not. Workarounds are invisible in process maps and invisible in requirements gathering, because the people who use them no longer see them as workarounds.

The rule: no workaround is carried onto the replacement platform without a recorded decision. Each item below ends in one of four dispositions, and the list of dispositions is deliverable B-08 to the Replacement Programme so that nothing is rebuilt by default.

DispositionMeaningWho decides
RETIREThe gap it covers no longer exists in the to-be process; nothing replaces itDesign Authority
NATIVEThe need is real; the replacement platform meets it as standard configuration (rule, timer, template, integration)Solution Architect confirms in fit-gap (2.7)
REBUILDThe need is real and the platform cannot meet it natively; a custom component or integration is requiredDesign Authority; goes on the custom list (O5)
KEEP (interim)Needed until a later release; retire date setReplacement Programme

Twenty-three workarounds trace to seven root causes. Four of the seven are standard capability in modern lending platforms; one (integrations) is Programme scope; two are design choices this project owns. Hypothesis D1 is supported in structure: every batch job and manual bridge identified maps to a missing rule, timer, task or integration. Bot-level confirmation (W-05) waits on DR-09.

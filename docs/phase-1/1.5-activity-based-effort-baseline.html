1. Purpose

Objective O2 (lower cost to serve, release capacity) can only be evidenced against a baseline of where effort goes today: which roles spend how much time on which activities, for which deal types. BLP does not record effort. The baseline therefore has to be constructed, and constructed in a way the SteerCo will accept at G1 and Finance will accept at G2.

Three sources are triangulated. None is sufficient alone: a survey over-reports “real work” and under-reports admin; time-and-motion observes too few people to generalise; system timestamps show elapsed time, not effort. Together they bound the answer.

Stratified by role and segment; within strata, random from DR-11 active users. Team leaders nominate alternates for absence. Minimum acceptable response: 60% per stratum (R-11).

Timing

Week 5, Monday to Friday (7–11 December 2026). Avoids month-end and the Christmas run-down. One week is a limitation; the reconciliation to DR-02 volumes (section 5, step 4) tests whether the week was representative.

Instrument

  • Daily form, 15 minutes, end of day: hours (to 0.25) against the 22 lines; must total the working day
  • Per line: number of deals touched (for A02–A15), to derive touch time per deal
  • One free-text box: “biggest time-waster today”
  • Anonymised at source: respondent ID → role × segment × week only
  • Delivered as a form link from the team leader (C-05), not from the project

Time-and-motion

Observed, not self-reported. Five activities selected by expected volume × touch time: (1) renewal submission preparation (A04/A13), (2) credit assessment of a renewal (A07), (3) facility letter production and issue (A09), (4) variation set-up (A03/A14), (5) fulfilment of a facility (A12). Target 8–12 observed instances each across two segments. Records: start/stop per step, system used, interruptions, re-keying.

Loaded cost

DR-16: fully loaded annual cost per role band from Finance. Converted to cost per productive hour using Finance’s standard productive-hours assumption (to be stated in the model, not assumed by the project).

5. Analysis steps (Week 6)

  1. Clean and scale. Remove incomplete days; scale each stratum to its DR-11 population; report response rate per stratum.
  2. Calibrate. For the five observed activities, compute survey touch-per-deal ÷ observed touch-per-deal. Expect 1.1–1.4 (survey over-reports). Apply the factor to the un-observed activities of the same role, and state it.
  3. Re-allocate deal-type lines. Split A13–A15 hours across L2 steps in proportion to S3 unit times for that deal type.
  4. Reconcile to volume. For each L2: annualised survey hours vs. DR-02 volume × unit time. Variance within ±20% → central estimate is the mean; outside → report both as the range and investigate (usually the survey week was atypical, or a step is performed outside BLP and not timestamped).
  5. Split touch and wait. From DR-02 elapsed per stage minus S3 touch, by deal type. This is the input to hypothesis C4 and to the cycle-time baseline in 1.4.
  6. Cost. Apply DR-16 rates. Produce cost per deal by deal type, and total annual cost of the lending process by role.
  7. Report ranges, not points. G1 endorses a range. Finance has agreed (0.9 counting rules) that the business case uses the central estimate with sensitivity at the bounds.

What the illustration shows about the method, not the Bank: even with New Money touch time at 2.6× a renewal, renewals and variations would carry about 70% of deal-tied effort because of volume.

1. How the pain points were found

A pain-point register is only as good as the method that produced it. Two failure modes are common: a list of complaints collected in workshops (loud, not representative) or a list of system defects (technical, not business). We used a structured approach with five sources, three lenses and a fixed question bank, so that every entry can be traced to where it came from and why it qualifies.

1.3 Qualification rule

An observation becomes a pain point only if it meets all three tests: (a) it is tied to a specific process step or rule, not a general complaint; (b) it has a plausible business consequence in at least one of five dimensions (cycle time, cost/effort, risk/control, customer experience, colleague experience); (c) it is distinguishable from a preference (“I would like a different screen layout” fails; “the landing page shows no work due so renewals start late” passes).

1.4 Scoring

Frequency (deals or events affected per year)< 100100–1,0001,000–5,0005,000–15,000> 15,000 or every deal
Impact (worst of the five dimensions)Minutes; cosmeticHours of effort or days of delay; no riskDays of delay or material effort; or MI distortionWeeks of delay, rework of a stage, or control weaknessRe-approval, regulatory or conduct exposure, or customer loss

Score = Frequency × Impact (1–25). Evidence strength is recorded separately and does not change the score; it determines how much validation the item needs. Scores are a ranking device for choosing root-cause themes, not a benefits estimate; sizing happens in 1.10.

2. Question bank used in discovery

The same questions were put to every role, in the same order, so that answers can be compared across RM, Credit, Lending Services, Fulfilment and SBS. Role-specific probes follow the core set. Interviewers record the step reference for every answer; an answer without a step reference goes to the parking lot.

2.1 Voice-of-business interview guide (45 min)

Opening (5 min). Your role, segment, how many deals you touch in a typical week, which deal types.
Walk a deal (15 min). Take the last renewal [variation / new money] you completed. From the moment you knew it was needed to the moment the customer could use the facility: what happened, in order? Where did it wait, and on whom? What did you do outside BLP? What did you have to do twice?
Time (10 min). Of your working week, roughly what share is on: new business, renewals and variations, chasing (customers, Credit, other teams), system admin and re-keying, customer relationship time not tied to a deal? What would you stop doing tomorrow if you could?
Controls (5 min). Which checks do you perform because policy requires them, and which because “that’s how it’s done”? Have you ever been unsure whether a deal was within your authority? What happens when a customer changes their mind after approval?
Failure (5 min). Think of a deal that went wrong or took far too long. What happened? What would have prevented it?
Close (5 min). If the new platform could do one thing BLP cannot, what would it be? Who else should we speak to?

5. Root-cause analysis

Ten themes cover the top-scoring pain points. For each: the effect (the pain as the business experiences it), a cause-and-effect (fishbone) diagram on six bones adapted to a bank process, a five-whys chain to the root, the root cause stated in one line, and the levers in the benefits tree (0.9) that address it.

BoneMeaning in a lending process
ProcessSequence, steps, loops, hand-offs as designed and as practised
Policy & ControlsCredit policy, delegated authority, control requirements and how they are expressed
SystemBLP capability and configuration; integrations; workarounds
DataWhat is captured, where, in what form; what is missing or wrong
People & SkillsJudgement, habit, capability, incentives at the individual level
Organisation & GovernanceOwnership, measurement, team boundaries, decision rights

Rule for the five whys: stop when the answer is something the project can change or must explicitly accept, not before. Several chains end at the same place (effort is unmeasured; policy lives in documents; the system has no concept of time), which is the point: fewer root causes than pain points.

Five whys

  1. Why do renewals take the full path? → Because the workflow has a single Deal & Credit path for every deal type.
  2. Why a single path? → Because BLP routes by deal type, not by risk, and deal type “Renewal” was built as a copy of New Money.
  3. Why not by risk? → Because no rules engine exists and the DA determination is manual, so routing cannot use data the system holds.
  4. Why is the determination manual? → Because the DA matrix is a document and nobody specified it as rules when BLP was configured.
  5. Why was it not specified? → Because the original implementation replicated the paper process (“paving the cow path”), which is the risk this project exists to avoid on the replacement.

Root cause

Routing is by deal type, not by risk; the delegated-authority policy was never expressed as system rules

L1, L3

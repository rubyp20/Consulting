### Slide 2 – Why we are here (Sponsor)
- BLP is being replaced. If we replace the screens but keep the process, we carry 11 deal types, four batch jobs, a set of bots and manual facility letters onto a new platform.
- 30,561 deals last year. Two-thirds were renewals and variations, where colleagues spend most time on steps that add nothing to the customer or to credit quality.
- This project designs the process the new platform will be built to. It runs 17 weeks to ready-to-implement, with the design and business case approved in 13.
- Three commitments: credit control is not weakened; capacity we free is a management decision made openly after the design; your time in workshops is how the design gets built around real work.

*Sponsor delivers this slide in their own words. Message 3 addresses the headcount concern directly (R-05).*

### Slide 7 – What makes this different from a generic process review
1. **Servicing first.** Design effort goes where the volume is.
2. **Fit-to-standard.** Adopt the platform's standard journey where good enough; custom only with a documented reason.
3. **Control equivalence.** No control removed or automated without a signed replacement. Second line is on the Design Authority from day one, not at the end.
4. **Route by risk, not by deal type.** Fast-track criteria co-designed with Credit Risk using our own data.
5. **A deliverable the Programme can build.** Requirements pack in the backlog by Week 13; high-impact decisions released Week 10.

---

### Slide 8 – Our starting hypotheses (to be tested, not assumed)
- A material share of renewals could be decided within delegated authority without full Credit review, with no increase in risk
- The seven mapped journeys share one Lead & Customer stage and a near-identical Deal & Credit flow; deal types differ by parameters, not by process
- Rework and wait, not touch time, dominate cycle time: deferrals, letter return, changes after approval
- Each bot and batch job covers a missing rule the new process should hold natively
- A share of control steps has no policy source

*Full tree in 0.6. We will report each as confirmed, partly confirmed or rejected at G1.*

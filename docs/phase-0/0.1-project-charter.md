## 1. Purpose of this charter

This charter is the single reference for what the project is, what it is not, how success is judged, and who decides. It is approved once at G0. Any change after G0 goes through change control (§11) and is recorded in the decision log, so that scope is not re-opened informally at later gates.

## 2. Background

Business Bank administers c. 30,500 business lending deals a year across 12 product families on the Business Lending Platform (BLP), used by c. 900 colleagues from Business Direct to Corporate. BLP is being replaced. The Bank has produced current-state journey maps for the replacement but has not yet defined a target-state process. Without one, the replacement will be configured to today's process, including its workarounds (Blue Prism bots, four batch jobs, off-platform facility letters) and its redundant deal types.

This project defines the target-state lending process, operating model and business case, and delivers a process requirements pack to the BLP Replacement Programme before its requirements baseline is frozen.

## 3. Objectives and success criteria

| # | Objective | Success criterion at G2 (design approved) | Success criterion at benefits realisation |
|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | Reduce time to decision and fulfilment, with priority on servicing journeys | Target cycle time per journey pattern set against the G1 baseline and accepted by SteerCo | Target cycle times achieved in pilot segment within 2 releases |
| O2 | Reduce cost to serve and release capacity | Capacity release by role quantified, with named benefit owners | ≥ 80% of signed benefit realised within 12 months of each release |
| O3 | Maintain or strengthen credit control | Every removed or automated control has a signed control-equivalence entry; zero unresolved second-line objections | No increase in policy exceptions or authority breaches post-implementation |
| O4 | Reduce rework | Rework loops identified, quantified and designed out or bounded | First-time-right rate improved against baseline by the agreed target |
| O5 | Simplify for the replacement | Deal types reduced to agreed journey patterns; Fit/Configure/Custom/Gap classification complete; requirements pack accepted into Programme backlog | Custom build on the replacement limited to items on the approved custom list |
| O6 | Protect regulatory and MI data | No regulatory reporting field removed without MI sign-off; data lineage documented | No regulatory reporting break at go-live |
| O7 | Improve colleague and customer experience | RM steps per journey reduced; measurement approach agreed | RM and customer measures improved against baseline |

**Headline target to be confirmed at G1 (indicative ranges only, from industry benchmarks, not yet from Bank data):** 30–50% reduction in end-to-end cycle time on fast-track renewals; 15–25% touch-time reduction across servicing journeys; deal types reduced from 11 to 5 patterns.

## 4. Scope

### 4.1 In scope
- Lending lifecycle processes administered on BLP: lead and opportunity, deal and credit, documentation and fulfilment, in-life servicing (Renewal, EPR, Variation, Remove All Facilities, change after approval), distressed handling (CAP Referral, CAP Strategy, in-flight referral), pre-approved drawdown (Shadow Limit, Shadow Limit Reallocation, Staged Drawdown, Staged Drawdown Allocation)
- Deposit opportunity recording
- All 12 product families; all 11 deal types including retirement decisions
- Segments: Business Direct, Strategic Finance, Corporate (depth per §5)
- Controls, delegated-authority rules and policy application embedded in the processes
- Workarounds around BLP: Blue Prism bots, the four batch jobs, off-platform documents, email issuance
- Roles: RMs, Business Direct, Credit, Lending Services, Fulfilment, SBS/PS, Business Operations, Corporate Associates, Credit Risk Policy, MI/Regulatory Reporting

### 4.2 Out of scope
- Personal Association Lend
- Platform selection, build, configuration, testing, data migration
- Credit policy content and risk appetite (application and controls are in scope; policy setting is not)
- Changes to MEO, IMPACS, TELLER, CIF Portal beyond interface requirements
- Personal lending; retail deposits

## 5. Scope decisions for G0

These four were left open in the RFP. The recommendation is recorded here; the SteerCo decision is recorded in the decision log (0.8) at G0.

| ID | Decision | Options | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SD-01 | ESR Cleanse (12.5% of volume) | (a) In scope as a process; (b) Out of scope, handled by Replacement data migration; (c) In scope only to specify what migration must preserve | **(c)** | It is a records transfer, not a customer deal. Keeping it in the deal list distorts volumes and the baseline. The project should specify the personal-exposure and NAG-exposure updates migration must preserve, then stop. |
| SD-02 | Corporate segment depth | (a) Full mapping and baselining; (b) Mapping only, via Corporate Associates as proxies, no restricted data; (c) Exclude | **(b)** | Insider-information barriers prevent partner access to live Corporate data. Journeys are structurally the same; segment differences can be captured through proxies without restricted data. |
| SD-03 | Deposit opportunity location | (a) Stays on lending platform; (b) Moves to CRM/pipeline tool; (c) Defer until Replacement platform's opportunity capability is confirmed | **(c), decide by G1** | 98 opportunities in progress is low volume; the right home depends on whether the replacement has a usable opportunity object. Deciding now risks a wrong answer. |
| SD-04 | Capacity release treatment | (a) Headcount reduction target; (b) Redeployment to revenue activity; (c) Quantify capacity only; treatment decided by line management post-G2 | **(c)** | Keeps the project credible with RMs and Credit during discovery (participation collapses if the project is seen as a headcount exercise). Business case shows capacity in FTE-equivalent and value at both treatments. |

## 6. Approach summary

Five phases, four gates. Detail in the Approach document (02) and the Integrated Plan (0.3).

| Phase | Weeks | Gate | Gate decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Mobilise | 1–2 | G0 | Charter, governance, plan approved |
| 1 Discover | 3–7 | G1 | Baseline endorsed as the reference for benefits |
| 2 Design | 8–13 | G2 | To-be design and business case approved; funding released; requirements pack accepted by Replacement Programme |
| 3 Plan Delivery | 14–17 | G3 | Ready to implement |
| 4 Implement, Embed, Track | 18+ | per release | Scale / no-scale per pilot and release |

Design principles that bind the approach: fit-to-standard against the replacement platform; no control removed without equivalence; route by risk not by deal type; capture once at source.

## 10. Assumptions and constraints

**Assumptions**
- Replacement platform selected, or selected by G1, so fit-gap is against a real product
- Current L3 maps available in editable form in Week 1
- BLP stage-transition timestamps extractable for the last 12 months
- Replacement Programme requirements freeze date is no earlier than Week 13; if earlier, see RAID R-01

**Constraints**
- No disruption to live lending operations; SME load agreed with line management
- Insider-information barriers for Corporate
- Regulatory reporting fields are protected
- MI data lead time 3–4 weeks

## 11. Change control

Changes to scope, objectives, gates or key dates after G0 require a change request to the SteerCo, with impact on plan, resource and benefits stated. Decisions are recorded in the decision log (0.8). Changes within a phase that do not affect scope or dates are agreed by the Engagement Manager and Bank PM and logged.
